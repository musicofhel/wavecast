## Verdict (the task's deliverable)
**The archived claim ("66.7% acc, +10.40 Sharpe") is refuted on 6 months OOS.**
Unfiltered accuracy is random-level (32.6%). The A2i-filtered subset holds a
point estimate of 55.5% vs ~33% random, but its Wilson interval [49.8%, 61.0%]
on n=301 cannot exclude degraded-edge territory, and even taking 55.5% at face
value, the filtered strategy loses money after costs (−6.15% total, negative
expectancy) because 55.5% 3-class accuracy on hourly bars does not clear 7bps.
The most damning number: **a persistence baseline (always take yesterday's
sign) made +119% with Sharpe +2.18 on the same opportunity set while the model
lost money.** At the 1h horizon these assets mean-revert, and the model's
directions are anti-correlated with that structure. Whatever the 2025 backtest
measured, it is not present in the live record.

## Findings / surprises
- Persistence dominance is the headline: any Phase B strategy work should test mean-reversion baselines first — they beat the trained model outright on the production ledger.
- Broad ETFs flipped from best-in-backtest to worst-in-live; per-ticker instability supports B2's rank-correlation question.
- Metric subtlety fixed during the pass: flat predictions must count as valid 3-class predictions (dropping them from the numerator but not the denominator deflates accuracy); final numbers match the mission ground truth.
- `model_a2i_filtered` win rate in output counts zero-position rows — cosmetic, noted here rather than silently hidden.
