## Findings / surprises
1. **Per-ticker backtest quality IS partially stable on daily bars** — Spearman
   rho ≈ +0.5 for persistence/mean-reversion at 1d (vs ~0 to −0.13 for daily
   momentum). On hourly the ranks correlate (+0.3..+0.7) but every ticker is a
   loser after costs (tr+=0 of 20), so stability there is stability of losing.
2. **Top-5-by-train selection beats the full universe OOS only for 1d
   persistence**: +0.38 vs −0.41 (≈+0.8 Sharpe spread). For every other config
   the spread is ≤ +0.15 or negative — i.e., no reliable universe-selection
   alpha except where the rule itself is already marginal.
3. **Selection risk is real even in the winning case**: UNG (train +0.64) went
   to −0.73 and AMZN (train +0.38) to −1.21 OOS; BAC carried the top-K result
   (+1.57). Top-K mean is not diversified — it is concentrated in whichever
   name kept its regime.
4. Hourly remains cost-dead regardless of which tickers you pick (confirms B1's
   "hourly per-bar rules are a cost floor" — now shown per-ticker, not just in
   aggregate).
5. Implication for B4: since selection adds little robust alpha, the lever is
   trade management (holding periods, fewer flips) rather than cherry-picking
   tickers.
